### The Aisle

On February 12, 1966, Barbara had to turn sideways to pass between the synagogue pews. The baby pressed low beneath her ribs; soon, everyone said. Soon, as if the child had a schedule.

Rabbi Adler was speaking when the young man stood. The first shot cracked through the sanctuary. Then another. The rabbi fell, and the room broke apart—people shouting, rushing for the doors, one another.

Barbara gripped the pew. The young man had turned the gun on himself. He was still there, alone amid the scattering crowd.

Someone had to reach him. He was only a boy. She pushed forward, one hand on her belly, the other reaching—but the fleeing people swept her backward. A shoulder struck hers; an arm caught her coat. The aisle vanished beneath a crush of bodies.

She wanted to shout that she could help. Couldn’t anyone see she was trying?

Outside, the winter air struck her face. Barbara stood with strangers, one hand over the child, listening for a sound from inside. The baby moved beneath her palm.

Soon, everyone said. But not this soon.
